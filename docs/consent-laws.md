# All-Party Consent Jurisdictions & Statutory Citations

*Last verified: September 2026*

Under American Bar Association Model Rule 8.4 and state ethics opinions, secretly recording conversations without the consent of all parties can violate legal ethics and statutory wiretapping laws.

The following US jurisdictions require all parties to consent to recording private or wire communications:

| State | Statutory Citation | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **California** | Cal. Penal Code § 632 | Prohibits recording confidential communications without consent of all parties. |
| **Connecticut** | Conn. Gen. Stat. § 52-570d | Requires consent of all parties for recording telephone conversations (with civil liability). |
| **Delaware** | 11 Del. C. § 1335 | Requires consent of all parties to intercept oral/wire communications. |
| **Florida** | Fla. Stat. § 934.03 | All parties must consent to recording oral or wire communications. |
| **Illinois** | 720 ILCS 5/14-2 | All-party consent for private conversations with reasonable expectation of privacy. |
| **Maryland** | Md. Code, Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 10-402 | Requires all parties to consent to oral and telephonic interception. |
| **Massachusetts** | Mass. Gen. Laws ch. 272 § 99 | Strict prohibition on recording oral communications without all parties' consent. |
| **Montana** | Mont. Code Ann. § 45-8-213 | Requires all-party consent unless explicit warning announcement is given. |
| **Nevada** | Nev. Rev. Stat. § 200.620 / § 200.650 | All-party consent required for wire communication interception. |
| **New Hampshire** | N.H. Rev. Stat. Ann. § 570-A:2 | All parties to communication must give consent. |
| **Oregon** | Or. Rev. Stat. § 165.540 | In-person conversations require notification/warning; telephonic requires one party. |
| **Pennsylvania** | 18 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 5704 | Two-party consent requirement for oral/wire communications. |
| **Washington** | Wash. Rev. Code § 9.73.030 | All-party consent mandatory for private communications. |
